<CaseStudySection title={"Why This, and Why Now?"}>
  <CaseStudyColumns layout={"split"}>
    <CaseStudyColumn side={"left"}>
      ### **A Gap Between Roadmaps and Strategy**

      When I joined Etsy's fulfillment team, I noticed a disconnect. Teams had near-term roadmaps and delivery targets, but they lacked a unifying vision — something that tied their work to Etsy's broader business strategy. Without that north star, it was difficult to make confident decisions or understand how each initiative contributed to the bigger picture.

      ### **Recognizing the Opportunity**

      I saw an opportunity to fill that gap. Not by imposing another rigid product plan, but by creating a shared vision grounded in user research, market realities, and Etsy's brand mission. This would give teams clarity on where we wanted to evolve, while still leaving room for flexibility in execution.

      ### **Goals of the Work**

      The purpose of this effort went beyond documentation. My goals were to:

      * **Create a vision** that reflected user needs while aligning with Etsy's business strategy.
      * **Define guideposts** that could shape yearly and quarterly planning, ensuring continuity between strategy and execution.
      * **Build consensus** across design, product, engineering, and business leaders that this work was both necessary and urgent.

      ### **Leading with Influence**

      Because this wasn't work that had been formally requested, success depended on influence and alignment. I initiated the effort, rallied cross-functional leadership, and created the conditions for the team to see the value in stepping back from delivery to define a shared direction.

<CaseStudySection title={"Workshop Design"}>
  <CaseStudyColumns layout={"split"}>
    <CaseStudyColumn side={"left"}>
      To build true alignment across disciplines, I designed a three-day workshop focused on co-creation, strategic thinking, and grounding our decisions in real user needs. The structure was intentional: each activity layered insight and perspective, ultimately enabling the team to converge on a shared direction.

      ### **Grounding in Research**

      We began by immersing ourselves in key inputs — seller behavior data, industry trends, brand positioning, and a reminder of Etsy’s broader mission — to ensure we were anchored in both user insight and business context.

      ### **Thinking Hats Exercise**

      To challenge assumptions and foster empathy, we explored seller opportunities through a set of creative mindsets — including the optimist, pessimist, and visionary — uncovering nuanced perspectives often missed in traditional brainstorming.

      ### **Future View**

      We envisioned what a best-in-class fulfillment experience could look like 3–5 years out, encouraging blue-sky thinking while staying tethered to seller and buyer realities.

      ### **Cover Story**

      We closed with a storytelling exercise where teams imagined Etsy featured in a future success story. This narrative served as a powerful foundation for the Fulfillment Mission I later authored and refined.

<CaseStudySection title={"Output: Fulfillment Mission"}>
  <CaseStudyColumns layout={"split"}>
    <CaseStudyColumn side={"left"}>
      By combining the output from the various workshop activities, especially the cover story exercise, I was able to write and refine the team’s thoughts into a mission statement. The team referred to this as the Fulfillment Mission.

      The resulting mission statement captured the heart of Etsy’s fulfillment journey: human-centered at its core, but aligned with the company’s broader mission and competitive realities. It became the shared voice of our leadership team and a touchstone for our direction.

      #### **“Commodity based ecommerce has put an emphasis on immediate delivery of items from a warehouse shelf to your door and has set this expectation with shoppers. Etsy’s buyers shop directly from independent sellers who make handmade items and souce vintage goods. The unique nature of our marketplace and the items being sold introduces an unfamiliar fulfillment process.**&#x20;

      #### **By providing a clear and trustworthy experience we can reset and establish meaningful expectations that are unique to Etsy. By doing this we can then redirect the focus on what matters most - the special”**
    </CaseStudyColumn>

<CaseStudySection title={"Output: Fulfillment Principles"} subheader={"Strategic Guideposts for Decision Making"}>
  <CaseStudyColumns layout={"split"}>
    <CaseStudyColumn side={"left"}>
      ### **Guiding Beyond Roadmaps**

      Our principles served as the scaffolding for roadmap planning. Unlike timelines or feature lists, they weren’t bound to a quarter or release cycle. Instead, they were evergreen beliefs that guided investment decisions and gave us a consistent lens for evaluating success.

      ### **Anchored in Seller Needs**

      Each principle was rooted in what mattered most to Etsy sellers — clarity, trust, and efficiency in how their orders were fulfilled. By grounding the work this way, we ensured that strategic discussions didn’t drift into abstract debates but stayed connected to the realities of our community.

      ### **A Leadership Tool**

      For me as a design leader, these principles became more than just a design artifact. They gave product, engineering, and business leaders a shared language to align around. In practice, they acted as decision-making guardrails, helping teams prioritize tradeoffs with confidence while keeping the seller experience at the center.

<CaseStudySection title={"Reflections & Takeaways"} subheader={"Leading Strategic Alignment in Cross-Functional Teams"}>
  <CaseStudyColumns layout={"split"}>
    <CaseStudyColumn side={"left"}>
      ### **What Worked Well**

      * **High ownership and alignment:** The workshop format empowered leaders from product, engineering, design, and operations to shape the output themselves. Because they co-authored the vision, they became natural advocates for it within their teams.
      * **Trust in a distributed setting:** Facilitating in real time created stronger connections across disciplines. It deepened trust and cohesion, which was especially important given Etsy’s distributed team structure.
      * **From artifact to action:** The vision and principles we created weren’t left on the shelf. They directly informed yearly planning cycles and long-term strategy discussions, proving the value of design-led alignment at the organizational level.

      ### **What I’d Improve Next Time**

      Time constraints limited how far we could explore “big bets” and visual prototypes for the future vision. Preserving space for this kind of exploration would have complemented the strategic framework with tangible inspiration — showing not just *where* we were headed, but *what it could look like in practice.*

      ### **My Takeaway as a Leader**

      For me, the biggest lesson was the power of creating space for co-creation. By facilitating, guiding, and amplifying the voices in the room, I shifted the work from *my* vision to *our* vision — a much stronger foundation for alignment and long-term momentum.
